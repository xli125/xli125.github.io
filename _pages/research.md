  <h1 id="research-title">Cancer evolution,<br>from genome to state<span>.</span></h1>
  <p class="research-intro">How do changes in the genome reshape the cellular states that cancer can access? My research connects genome instability, chromatin organization, and cell identity to understand how tumors adapt to therapy.</p>
  <div class="research-context"><span>Primary focus</span><p>Advanced prostate cancer · Therapeutic resistance · Lineage plasticity</p></div>

<section class="site-section" aria-labelledby="directions-title">
  <div class="section-heading"><h2 id="directions-title">Three connected research directions</h2><span class="section-note">Independent research vision</span></div>
  <div class="direction-list">
    <article id="genome-instability" class="direction"><span class="direction-number">01</span><div><p class="eyebrow">Origins of genome plasticity</p><h3>Where does evolutionary diversity begin?</h3><p>I investigate how endogenous mutational processes, including APOBEC activity and DNA damage, generate genetic and structural diversity. A central question is how these processes contribute to extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA) formation and cancer evolution.</p><div class="research-tags"><span>APOBEC</span><span>DNA damage</span><span>ecDNA biogenesis</span></div></div></article>
    <article id="cell-state" class="direction"><span class="direction-number">02</span><div><p class="eyebrow">Genome-to-state evolution</p><h3>How does genome architecture shape cell identity?</h3><p>My research aims to define how dynamic genome structures and chromatin organization influence transcriptional programs, heterogeneity, and lineage identity. Experimentally controlled systems help distinguish causal mechanisms from associations.</p><div class="research-tags"><span>Genome architecture</span><span>Chromatin</span><span>Lineage plasticity</span></div></div></article>
    <article id="therapeutic-adaptation" class="direction"><span class="direction-number">03</span><div><p class="eyebrow">Intercepting cancer evolution</p><h3>Can the capacity to evolve become a vulnerability?</h3><p>My long-term goal is to identify mechanisms and therapeutic strategies that limit genomic diversification, stabilize cell identity, or selectively target highly plastic states. This direction connects fundamental cancer biology with approaches to constrain therapeutic escape.</p><div class="research-tags"><span>Therapy resistance</span><span>Chemical biology</span><span>Cell-state vulnerabilities</span></div></div></article>
  </div>
